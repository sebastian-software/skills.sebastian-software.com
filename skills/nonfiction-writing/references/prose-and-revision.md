# Prose and Revision

Revision turns intention into an effect the reader can actually experience.
Work from meaning and structure toward sentences; otherwise polished lines can
protect a confused piece from necessary change.

## Establish a voice target

Describe the desired voice with observable qualities:

- direct or exploratory
- warm or restrained
- compact or expansive
- conversational or formal
- technically precise or broadly accessible
- personally present or deliberately backgrounded

Use samples from the author when available. Preserve recurring vocabulary,
sentence movement, humor, and perspective that belong to the person. Remove
accidental habits that obscure meaning; do not sterilize the prose into a
generic professional register.

## Prefer words that carry weight

- Choose a concrete noun over a vague category when the detail is known.
- Choose a verb that names the action instead of a weak verb plus an abstract
  noun: “decided” often carries more than “made a decision.”
- Keep adjectives and adverbs that change meaning; remove those that merely ask
  the reader to feel impressed.
- Replace jargon when a familiar word is equally accurate. Retain domain terms
  that compress real shared knowledge, and define them for outsiders.
- Examine fashionable phrases, clichés, and metaphors for the thought they may
  be concealing.
- Prefer positive statements when possible, while preserving meaningful
  negation and contrast.

Shorter is not automatically clearer. A qualification, example, or connecting
sentence may add words while reducing the reader's work.

## Shape sentences for meaning

Put familiar context near the beginning and important new information where it
receives emphasis. Keep subjects and verbs close enough that the reader does
not have to hold a long interruption in memory.

Use active voice when the actor and responsibility matter:

> The team changed the threshold.

Use passive voice when the result deserves focus or the actor is genuinely
unknown or irrelevant:

> The threshold was changed before the archived data was restored.

Vary sentence length and construction. A long sentence can carry accumulation,
qualification, or movement. A short one can land a consequence. Repetition can
create rhythm or clarity when intentional; accidental repetition dulls both.

Read paragraphs for sound:

- Where does the sentence naturally breathe?
- Does emphasis land on the important word?
- Do several sentences begin or end the same way without purpose?
- Does punctuation clarify the thought or decorate it?
- Would a reader stumble over an unintended echo or tongue-twisting cluster?

## Cut clutter without cutting the writer

Look for:

- openings that delay the subject
- phrases that repeat an idea already present
- abstract nouns surrounding a simple action
- qualifiers that express nervousness rather than accuracy
- stacked examples after the point is established
- meta-commentary about what the piece is about to say
- conclusions repeated at paragraph, section, and article level

Retain:

- qualifications required by the evidence
- details that make an example credible
- deliberate pauses, humor, and idiosyncratic phrasing
- context a new reader genuinely needs
- repetition that creates structure or emotional force

## Revise in passes

### 1. Meaning

- What is the piece actually saying?
- Has the governing idea shifted during drafting?
- Is any conclusion stronger than the supplied evidence?

### 2. Structure

- Is the reader's most important question answered at the right time?
- Can sections be moved, combined, or removed?
- Are examples doing analytical work or only decorating the piece?

### 3. Evidence

- Are facts, quotations, numbers, and outcomes supplied and attributable?
- Are interpretation and opinion presented as such?
- Are uncertainty and exceptions close to the claims they qualify?

### 4. Voice and audience

- Does the prose sound like this author addressing this reader?
- Does it explain what the reader needs without condescension?
- Has polish removed useful personality or honest tension?

### 5. Lines

- Can subjects, verbs, references, and relationships be understood on first
  reading?
- Can clutter be removed without losing meaning or cadence?
- Are pronouns, comparisons, and modifiers unambiguous?

### 6. Rhythm and mechanics

- Vary sentence and paragraph movement where monotony is accidental.
- Check spelling, grammar, punctuation, headings, names, figures, links, and
  formatting.
- Read aloud when possible, or simulate the pauses and emphasis closely.

### 7. Entry and exit

- Does the title make an honest promise?
- Does the lead enter at the right moment?
- Does the ending leave the intended implication or action?

## Give a useful critique

Prioritize feedback by consequence:

1. factual or evidentiary risk
2. mismatch between purpose and governing idea
3. missing, misplaced, or disproportionate sections
4. audience and voice problems
5. recurring sentence-level problems
6. isolated preferences

Explain the reader effect and offer a concrete revision direction. Separate
errors from tradeoffs and personal taste. A critique should make the next
revision easier, not prove that the critic can rewrite every sentence.

## Revision Check

- Is every consequential claim supportable?
- Can the main line be followed on a first reading?
- Are verbs, actors, and relationships specific?
- Has compression preserved nuance and voice?
- Does the prose sound intentional rather than uniformly polished?
- Have the title, lead, and ending been revised after the body?
