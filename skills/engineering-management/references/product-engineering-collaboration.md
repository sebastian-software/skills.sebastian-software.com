# Product, Design, and Engineering Collaboration

Use this reference when cross-functional responsibilities, discovery,
technical leadership, planning, or delivery collaboration are the problem.

## Share the Outcome, Separate the Contributions

Product, design, and engineering should share customer access, relevant
evidence, outcome accountability, and important tradeoffs. Their distinct
contributions remain visible:

- product leadership develops context about customers, business, value,
  priority, and product coherence;
- design develops evidence about comprehension, interaction, accessibility, and
  the experienced system;
- engineering develops evidence about feasibility, quality, operations,
  security, evolution, and delivery constraints;
- management designs the environment, capacity, responsibility, and feedback
  system that lets those judgments meet.

Do not make one discipline a service desk for another. Do not use shared
ownership to leave final decisions unnamed.

## Frame Work for an Empowered Team

A usable assignment includes:

1. target customer or user and their relevant progress;
2. outcome and evidence baseline;
3. commitments and non-negotiable constraints;
4. decision space owned by the team;
5. product, design, engineering, and operational evidence required;
6. time or investment boundary;
7. review and escalation conditions.

If leadership has already committed a feature or deadline, state that honestly.
Find the remaining decision space instead of relabeling delivery as discovery.

## Integrate Discovery and Delivery

Use a continuing loop:

- expose the riskiest value, usability, feasibility, viability, trust, and
  distribution assumptions;
- choose cheap, decision-relevant evidence;
- involve the disciplines needed to interpret it;
- make or escalate the decision;
- carry evidence and constraints into implementation;
- observe released behavior and revise.

Prototypes, estimates, technical spikes, and internal enthusiasm answer
different questions. None alone proves customer value.

## Clarify Technical Leadership

Technical leadership may sit with an individual contributor, manager, or
several bounded owners. Clarify:

- architecture and implementation decision domains;
- operational and quality responsibilities;
- consultation and escalation paths;
- mentoring and knowledge-spreading expectations;
- capacity explicitly allocated to this work.

Do not make the most senior engineer an unbounded approval queue. Route system
boundary and evolutionary architecture choices to `software-architecture`.

## Plan without False Precision

Connect planning to evidence and capacity:

- distinguish fixed commitments from forecasts and options;
- expose dependencies, uncertainty, quality work, and operational load;
- sequence learning before high-cost commitment;
- set decision checkpoints instead of manufacturing certainty;
- change scope, time, or resources explicitly when evidence moves.

Managers should remove system constraints and negotiate tradeoffs, not pressure
teams into privately absorbing them.

## Review the Collaboration System

Look for:

- one discipline controlling customer access or problem framing;
- product outcomes assigned without authority;
- design engaged only after scope is fixed;
- engineering consulted only for estimates;
- technical risk used to override value without shared inquiry;
- meetings that repeat decisions because no owner or record exists;
- managers resolving every cross-functional disagreement themselves.

Choose the smallest working agreement or decision-right change that addresses
the observed failure. Include a review date and affected-party feedback.

## Deliverable

Return the shared outcome, distinct responsibilities, decision rights,
evidence loop, commitments, capacity and risk constraints, escalation path,
and review cadence.
