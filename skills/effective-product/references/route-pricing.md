# Route: Pricing and Packaging

Turn incomplete market, customer, product, and financial evidence into a
testable pricing decision. Keep research findings, economic assumptions, package
design, and rollout choices separate so a polished price table does not hide
unsupported claims.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision before choosing numbers:
   - product, target segment, market, currency, and buying context
   - new offer, periodic review, test, or existing-customer migration
   - accountable owner, decision date, rollout authority, and reversibility
   - product promise, primary value event, alternatives, and business objective
   - known cost, margin, contractual, billing, support, and operational limits
2. Build an evidence register. Separate observed behavior, transaction and
   usage data, direct customer accounts, researched competitor facts,
   stakeholder beliefs, modeled assumptions, and unknowns. Never turn missing
   evidence into confident buyer language, market rates, or willingness to pay.
3. Load [Research and evidence](research-and-evidence.md) for willingness-to-pay
   research, current competitor checks, evidence strength, and a proportionate
   research plan. Use it before recommending exact prices when decision-relevant
   evidence is thin.
4. Load [Value metrics and models](value-metrics-and-models.md) when selecting
   the charged unit, comparing subscription, usage, seat, outcome, or hybrid
   models, or checking how customer value and product economics change across
   usage levels.
5. Load [Packaging and changes](packaging-and-changes.md) for package
   architecture, feature or usage fences, trials, discounts, rollout, and
   existing-customer migration. Do not assume the answer is three tiers.
6. Develop two to four materially different options. For each, show:
   - target customer and buying situation
   - metric, model, package structure, and price hypothesis
   - expected value alignment and business effect
   - evidence, assumptions, failure modes, and operational burden
   - validation method, decision window, guardrails, and rollback path
7. Recommend one option only at the precision supported by the evidence.
   Preserve plausible alternatives when uncertainty remains material.
8. Define the smallest ethical test or research step that can change the
   decision. State the signal, comparison, sample limits, decision rule, owner,
   and next review date without inventing universal thresholds.
9. For an approved change, define billing behavior, entitlements, customer
   cohorts, notices, support readiness, monitoring, exceptions, and rollback
   before launch.
10. Record durable rationale, rejected options, evidence date, assumptions, and
    reopening conditions through the Decisions route when the project maintains
    shared decision records.

## Operating Rules

- Price the value context, not the effort spent building the product.
- Treat a value metric as a product contract. It must be understandable,
  measurable, predictable enough to budget, hard to game, and aligned with
  value across relevant customer sizes.
- Distinguish willingness to pay from ability to pay, stated preference from
  behavior, and a competitor's published list price from an actual transaction.
- Use competitor pricing as context, not as permission to copy their model,
  packages, discounts, or price points.
- Model customer bills and business outcomes across realistic low, typical, and
  high usage. Surface cliffs, surprise bills, unbounded exposure, and perverse
  incentives.
- Keep package differences connected to distinct needs, value, service cost, or
  purchase process. Do not create arbitrary feature fences solely to make a
  comparison table look complete.
- Treat discounts, credits, trials, guarantees, and grandfathering as explicit
  economic and operational policies. Name their eligibility, duration, owner,
  abuse risk, and exit behavior.
- Protect existing customers from silent loss of purchased value, unexpected
  charges, broken workflows, and ambiguous migration promises.
- Do not optimize near-term revenue or conversion at the expense of trust,
  accessibility, truthful communication, or durable customer value.
- Label estimates, scenarios, and recommendations honestly. Never fabricate
  customer quotes, transactions, competitor facts, cost data, or research
  certainty.

## Default Deliverable

For a consequential pricing or packaging decision, return:

1. Decision scope, audience, authority, and constraints
2. Evidence register with dates, provenance, strength, and unknowns
3. Value hypothesis and candidate value metrics
4. Two to four model and package options with customer and business scenarios
5. Recommendation, confidence, assumptions, and rejected alternatives
6. Research or test plan with guardrails and decision rules
7. Rollout or migration plan, including notices, support, exceptions, and
   rollback
8. Measurement plan and conditions that reopen the decision

## Cross-links

- The target customer, product promise, strategy, scope, or viability question a
  pricing decision depends on is resolved on the Discovery, Strategy, and Scope
  routes.
- Fieldwork execution for willingness-to-pay research is the Research route.
- Positioning, messaging, proof, launch planning, sales enablement, and the
  persuasive expression of approved prices, packages, billing terms, discounts,
  trials, guarantees, eligibility, and migration constraints belong to
  `effective-marketing`. This route supplies the approved facts, evidence
  limits, package rationale, and migration constraints; that discipline does not
  change the underlying policy.
- Website disclosures, consent, privacy, and jurisdiction-specific legal
  requirements connected to presenting or testing an offer belong to
  `effective-web`.
- Tax, accounting, revenue-recognition, antitrust, regulated-pricing, and
  jurisdiction-specific contract questions require qualified specialists and
  remain outside this discipline.
