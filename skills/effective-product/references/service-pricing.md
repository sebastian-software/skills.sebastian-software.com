# Service Pricing and Expansion

## Establish a viable price

Estimate the customer's economic or personal value, direct delivery costs, staff
time, acquisition effort, risk, and required margin. In B2B, the buyer's
available budget and economic upside may help frame a price. In consumer
markets, affordability and perceived value may matter more. Competitor prices
are reference points, not instructions.

Build three cost blocks before recommending a number:

| Block | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Delivery | Hours by role, contractors, client management, revisions, tools |
| Acquisition | Prospecting, sales time, campaign cost, payment processing |
| Risk | Cost variation, nonpayment, added support, delays |

Calculate a floor below which the offer cannot remain viable. Compare it with
the plausible value to the customer and real alternatives. If the floor exceeds
likely willingness to pay, change the segment, scope, or delivery model. Do not
fix the problem by assuming an unproven premium position.

## Choose a payment structure

- **Fixed project:** appropriate when scope and acceptance can be defined.
  Include change requests and handoff.
- **Installments:** change cash flow and collection risk, not the underlying
  scope.
- **Ongoing service:** requires recurring value, a service cadence, limits,
  reporting, and exit terms.
- **Tiered offer:** an entry tier solves a narrow problem; later tiers extend
  the result. Each tier must make sense on its own.

When facts are missing, give a range with stated assumptions and the next market
check. Do not import hardcoded prices from an older example or another country.
Confirm local tax, invoicing, and contract treatment for the actual market.

## Decide whether to expand the account

An expansion offer is appropriate when the initial goal has been achieved or a
new bottleneck has become clear. Ask what problem follows the first improvement,
what distinct work would solve it, what evidence shows fit, and what resources
the customer would need to contribute. Introduce a possible next phase during
service planning without assuming the customer will buy. Do not price an
extension while delivery problems or trust gaps in the current engagement remain
unresolved.
