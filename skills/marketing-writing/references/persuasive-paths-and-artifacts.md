# Persuasive Paths and Artifacts

A persuasive path is an ordered set of reader questions. Choose the order that
fits the offer, entry state, evidence, and commitment instead of applying a
named formula mechanically.

## Build the belief and action path

A useful full path may cover:

1. **Recognition:** Is this for someone in my situation?
2. **Relevance:** Why does this matter now, and what is at stake?
3. **Progress:** What better state becomes possible?
4. **Mechanism:** How does the offer create that progress?
5. **Difference:** Why choose this over the current alternative?
6. **Proof:** What makes each consequential claim credible?
7. **Fit and risk:** What constraints, objections, effort, and switching costs
   should I understand?
8. **Action:** What proportionate step can I take, and what happens next?

This is a diagnostic inventory, not a mandatory page order. A referred reader
may need mechanism and fit before a problem restatement. A familiar low-risk
offer may require a direct action near the top. A novel or high-risk offer may
need category context, explanation, and proof before an invitation.

Write one sentence for the belief each section should establish. Remove
sections that repeat the same job. Add transitions that expose the reasoning,
not merely phrases that disguise a jump.

## Blend narrative and analytical evidence

Narrative helps the reader simulate a situation, notice change, and remember a
consequence. Analytical explanation helps the reader inspect mechanism,
comparison, conditions, and proof.

Use a blend when both recognition and scrutiny matter:

```text
recognizable moment -> concrete consequence -> explanatory mechanism
-> evidence or demonstration -> applicable boundary -> next choice
```

Lean more narrative when the experience, identity, aspiration, or use occasion
is central. Lean more analytical when the offer is utilitarian, technical,
expensive, regulated, unfamiliar, or hard to reverse. Do not invent scenes or
customer stories to compensate for missing evidence.

## Join claim and proof

For each consequential claim, record:

```text
claim -> required proof -> available proof -> material limit -> placement
```

Place proof where doubt arises. A logo strip cannot prove a performance
outcome, and a later case study may not rescue an unsupported hero claim.
Translate evidence at its actual strength:

- demonstration for capability;
- attributed case for one experience;
- measured result for a bounded outcome;
- comparison for a defined alternative and conditions;
- process transparency for how work happens;
- guarantee only when its policy is approved and operationally real.

## Write useful calls to action

A call to action should name the step or its value and reduce uncertainty about
what follows. Match commitment to evidence and relationship:

- inspect: view a demonstration, example, comparison, or documentation;
- learn: read a guide, calculate fit, or check requirements;
- try: start a reversible trial, sample, or guided setup;
- talk: request an assessment, consultation, or sales conversation;
- commit: buy, subscribe, apply, or migrate when conditions are clear.

Do not make vague button text carry an unclear offer. Use secondary actions only
for distinct reader needs, and keep their hierarchy visible.

## Adapt the path by artifact

### Homepage

Help several legitimate visitor intents orient without dissolving the position.
Lead with the broadest truthful promise that remains specific, establish what
the organization offers, show paths by meaningful situation, and give each path
relevant proof and action.

### Landing or sales page

Match the incoming promise, keep one primary conversion purpose, complete the
necessary argument on the page, and remove navigation or repetition only when
doing so does not obstruct trust, accessibility, or informed choice.

### Product, service, or feature page

Connect a recognizable situation to capability, mechanism, consequence, proof,
fit, and next step. Explain enough of the mechanism that the benefit does not
read as magic. Preserve technical and operational constraints.

### Pricing or package page

Help an eligible reader understand who each option fits, what value or usage
changes the choice, what is included, the actual billing conditions, and what
happens next. Do not invent tier rationale, discounts, guarantees, or urgency.

### About or trust page

Make origin, purpose, experience, operating principles, and relevance to the
reader concrete. A founder story should earn trust through decisions and
evidence, not synthetic vulnerability or a résumé in paragraphs.

### Campaign or launch asset

Keep the audience, promise, proof, terminology, and next action consistent
across touchpoints. Vary the angle only when it serves a distinct reader state;
do not let channel novelty rewrite the position or exceed the evidence.

### Marketing email

Respect the relationship and reason for contact. Make the subject and opening
accurately predict the message, deliver value before or alongside the ask,
state the action clearly, and avoid reply-like deception, false urgency, or
withheld material conditions.

## Final path check

- Does the opening recognize the entry state rather than restart the entire
  category story?
- Can the reader follow why each section comes next?
- Does every capability connect through a credible mechanism to progress?
- Is the real alternative represented fairly?
- Does proof appear where the corresponding doubt arises?
- Are material fit limits and costs visible before commitment?
- Is the emotional intensity proportional to the real stakes?
- Does the next step say what the reader receives or what happens next?
