## Diagnose from Work, Not Titles

Trace one consequential item from intent through decision, execution, and
feedback. For each step record:

- the desired outcome and evidence available;
- who recommends, decides, executes, advises, and must be informed;
- the rule or constraint governing the decision;
- the queue, approval, dependency, or interruption that delays it;
- the point where new evidence can change the course.

Do not solve an unclear decision by adding everyone as an approver. A named
decision owner needs a bounded domain, relevant inputs, escalation conditions,
and accountability for communicating the result.

## Design Responsibility

A responsibility is usable when it states:

1. the outcome or decision owned;
2. the authority included and excluded;
3. required collaborators and evidence;
4. escalation triggers;
5. feedback and review cadence.

Check for common gaps:

- accountability without authority;
- authority without context or affected-party input;
- multiple final decision makers;
- a responsibility split across incompatible incentives;
- unowned cross-team work;
- a manager who delegates tasks but retains every judgment call.

## Review a Decision Path

For a representative recent decision:

1. identify the first moment the decision could have been made;
2. list every handoff and wait;
3. mark which input materially changed the result;
4. remove ceremonial approval or duplicate synthesis;
5. place the decision near the best information while preserving governance;
6. publish the result and its reconsideration trigger.

Use durable decision records only when the choice must outlive the operating
conversation. Do not turn every reversible working choice into governance.

## Design Meetings as Tools

For each recurring meeting write:

- purpose and decision or shared understanding produced;
- owner, necessary participants, and optional observers;
- required inputs and preparation;
- why synchronous interaction is valuable;
- output, distribution, and next review date.

Keep synchronous time for conflict resolution, sensitive feedback, ambiguous
discovery, incident learning, or fast joint decisions when asynchronous
exchange would prolong or impoverish the work. Shorten, split, make optional,
move asynchronous, or cancel sessions whose purpose and output cannot be
stated.

Do not optimize only for meeting hours. A canceled coordination point can
create longer waits, private rework, or risk shifted to less powerful people.

## Choose the coordination medium from the work

Diagnose whether the work needs information transfer, asynchronous review,
rapid convergence, tacit-knowledge transfer, ambiguous co-creation, conflict
resolution, trust repair, or relationship building. Choose the least costly
medium that preserves the necessary richness: a durable document, asynchronous
comments, a call, pairing, a workshop, or in-person collaboration.

Use in-person time only with a named outcome, necessary participants, prepared
inputs, an accessible and equitable location and schedule, and a durable output
or follow-up owner. Account for travel, caregiving, disability, timezone,
environmental, and concentration costs. Do not prescribe a universal onsite
cadence or treat physical presence as evidence of commitment. Review whether
the chosen medium reduced delay, misunderstanding, rework, or relationship risk.

## Establish a Management Cadence

Choose the minimum cadence that closes the loop:

- brief operational review for flow, risks, and constraints;
- one-to-ones for the individual's agenda, support, feedback, and growth;
- product and technical reviews for evidence and consequential decisions;
- periodic team-system review for load, dependencies, and role clarity.

The cadence is a hypothesis. Give it an owner, start date, signals, and a date
to keep, adjust, or stop it.

## Measure Carefully

Combine outcome and human evidence:

- decision lead time and reversals;
- blocked time and dependency queues;
- escaped quality or operational risk;
- focus time and interruption sources;
- clarity reported by affected people;
- workload sustainability and participation quality.

Never use activity volume, online status, or private-message inspection as a
substitute for contribution. Aggregate sensitive signals and collect only what
the decision genuinely needs.

## Deliverable

Return the current work path, responsibility gaps, smallest operating change,
safeguards, owner, review signals, and conditions for escalation.
