# Product Marketing

Turn verified product and market evidence into a position people can understand,
claims they can trust, a coordinated route to market, and a learning loop that
can revise the choices. Keep product facts, positioning, message expression, and
channel execution separately reviewable.

## Workflow

1. Define the decision and artifact:
   - positioning or segment choice
   - category decision
   - message or claim system
   - launch and sales-enablement plan
   - distribution motion or build-versus-distribution diagnosis
   - services-to-product scaling choice
   - win/loss or adoption review
2. Establish the evidence boundary. Separate:
   - verified product behavior, constraints, and customer outcomes
   - observed customer situations, alternatives, language, and commitments
   - market and competitor facts with source and date
   - interpretations, assumptions, open questions, and unsupported claims

   Never fill a missing row with invented research, quotations, differentiation,
   market size, competitor behavior, or product certainty.
3. Keep four layers distinct:

   ```text
   product evidence -> market position -> message expression -> channel tactic
   ```

   Do not use polished copy or channel response as retroactive proof that the
   product works or that the position is true.
4. Load only the matching route:
   - [Positioning and segmentation](references/positioning-and-segmentation.md)
     for target-segment choices, alternatives, category context,
     differentiation, positioning options, and the positioning brief.
   - [Messaging and proof](references/messaging-and-proof.md) for message
     architecture, claim-to-proof checks, audience adaptation, objections,
     calls to action, and proof gaps.
   - [Launch and enablement](references/launch-and-enablement.md) for launch
     scope, readiness, sequencing, ownership, sales enablement, competitive
     context, rollout guardrails, measurement, distribution-motion selection,
     build-versus-distribution diagnosis, and services-to-product scaling.
   - [Market learning](references/market-learning.md) for win/loss work,
     adoption analysis, interviews, evidence synthesis, and decisions about
     changing the segment, position, message, proof, or channel.
5. Make two to four materially different choices when the direction is still
   open. Compare their audience specificity, customer relevance, evidence,
   tradeoffs, constraints, and cost of being understood.
6. Select one recommendation only at the confidence supported by the evidence.
   Name non-goals, disqualifying evidence, and what would reopen the decision.
7. Translate the approved position into the minimum downstream handoff:
   audience, situation, alternatives, differentiated value, supported claims,
   proof, constraints, tone boundaries, and desired next action.
8. Define the learning loop before execution. Name the signal, eligible
   audience, evidence window, guardrails, owner, and keep, change, or stop rule.

## Operating Rules

- Start from a recognizable customer situation and current alternative, not a
  broad demographic label or an imagined persona.
- Treat a segment as useful only when the people in it share decision-relevant
  needs, alternatives, buying conditions, or response to the product.
- Choose category language for comprehension and truthful comparison. Creating
  a new category carries an education cost and does not itself differentiate
  the product.
- Make differentiation specific, relevant to the selected audience, supported
  by product mechanism and proof, and defensible against the real alternative.
- Treat customer wording as evidence only when it is supplied with enough
  context to understand who said it, when, and about what experience.
- Make every consequential outcome, comparison, speed, ease, universality, or
  superiority claim traceable to current evidence and material constraints.
- Use proof proportional to the claim and audience risk. A feature screenshot
  cannot establish a customer outcome; one anecdote cannot establish a
  universal result.
- Preserve qualifications that keep a claim true. Do not strip limits from a
  message merely to make it shorter or more forceful.
- For technical products and projects, keep approved technical terms stable and
  translate the verified mechanism into buyer relevance without changing its
  behavior, conditions, or constraints.
- Treat launch as a coordinated change in market exposure and operations, not
  a fixed ceremony or a content calendar.
- Equip sales to discover fit, show supported value, handle uncertainty, and
  record objections. Do not give them fabricated competitor facts, guaranteed
  outcomes, or authority the product cannot honor.
- Study wins, losses, non-decisions, failed adoption, and retained use. Do not
  let a convenient sample or a desired narrative decide what the market said.
- Keep platform behavior, channel performance, competitive facts, and market
  conditions dated and verifiable. Recheck volatile inputs before relying on
  them.

## Default Deliverable

For a broad product-marketing decision, return:

1. Decision, audience, market boundary, owner, and evidence cutoff
2. Evidence register with important contradictions and unknowns
3. Selected segment, situation, alternatives, and category choice
4. Positioning recommendation, options rejected, and non-goals
5. Message hierarchy, supported claims, proof, constraints, and proof gaps
6. Launch and enablement handoffs with readiness conditions and ownership
7. Market-learning plan with signals, guardrails, and decision window
8. Conditions that would change the segment, position, message, or launch

## Routing Boundaries

- Use `market-research` when a segment, category, positioning, message, launch,
  or market-learning decision requires a new customer or buyer research
  program, recruitment, win/loss fieldwork, review or community research,
  market sizing, or cross-source synthesis. Pass the decision and evidence
  gaps; receive buyer language, alternatives, decision criteria, proof gaps,
  and evidence limits without delegating the market-facing decision.
- Use `product-management` to establish or revise the target user, customer
  problem, product thesis, scope, product evidence, quality bar, and release
  decision. Return market response and adoption evidence there when it changes
  the product decision.
- Use `pricing-and-packaging` for willingness-to-pay research, value metrics,
  pricing models, packages, discounts, trials, and customer migrations. This
  skill communicates only approved commercial facts and their evidence limits.
- Use `marketing-writing` to turn an approved audience, position, message
  hierarchy, claim-and-proof register, offer, constraints, tone boundaries, and
  desired action into homepages, landing pages, product or service pages,
  pricing pages, campaigns, launches, sales assets, marketing emails, and
  marketing-specific copy revisions. Return message and proof gaps here.
- Use `conversion-optimization` to diagnose funnel constraints and design
  ethical tests of an approved position, message, proof, offer expression, or
  channel path. Return evidence here when it changes the segment, position,
  claim, proof, launch, or channel decision.
- Use `nonfiction-writing` to plan, draft, and revise articles, editorial case
  studies, thought leadership, public project narratives, and other primarily
  informative or argumentative long-form prose after the audience, position,
  claims, proof, purpose, approved technical terminology, and material
  constraints are stable enough to brief.
- Use `create-social-content` to express an approved position, message, launch,
  proof point, or learning across non-LinkedIn or multiple social platforms.
  Pass the selected audience, situation, claim, proof, constraints, and desired
  next action without letting channel response redefine the position as fact.
- Use `effective-web` to specify, implement, and verify landing pages, product
  pages, forms, experiments, analytics delivery, accessibility, performance,
  and responsive behavior after the market-facing requirements are clear.
- Use `originality-review` when a produced campaign or market-facing experience
  must be compared with supplied references for copied identity, content,
  claims, media, or distinctive combinations. Return here to correct the
  approved position, message, proof, and campaign brief.
- Use `linkedin-social-selling` when LinkedIn is an evidence-backed channel for
  profiles, relationships, content systems, conversations, lead magnets, or
  pipeline. Pass it the approved position, messages, proof, and learning goal.
- Use `web-legal-compliance` to scope current jurisdiction-specific
  requirements for claims, testimonials, endorsements, direct marketing,
  privacy, consent, tracking, disclosures, and online sales.
- Product strategy, legal conclusions, commercial or editorial prose, web
  implementation, and channel operation remain with their named owners; do not
  absorb them into a product-marketing deliverable.
