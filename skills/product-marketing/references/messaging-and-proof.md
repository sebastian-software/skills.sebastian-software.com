# Messaging and Proof

Use this route to translate an approved position into an understandable message
system and to keep every consequential claim proportional to its evidence.

## Confirm the inputs

Before drafting, require:

- target audience, situation, and current alternative
- chosen category context
- differentiated value and product mechanism
- verified product behavior and constraints
- supplied customer evidence and language
- intended action, channel context, and risk level

If these inputs remain open, return to positioning or route the unresolved
product decision to `product-management`. Do not use persuasive language to
hide an unstable strategy.

## Build a claim-to-proof register

For every consequential claim, record:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | For whom is the claim intended? |
| Situation | Under which trigger or condition should it hold? |
| Claim | What exactly is being asserted? |
| Mechanism | Which verified product behavior could make it true? |
| Product evidence | Has that behavior been verified in the supported experience? |
| Customer proof | Did representative customers reach or commit to the outcome? |
| Constraint | Where, when, or for whom does it not hold? |
| Status | Supported, partial, unsupported, contradicted, or unknown |
| Action | Keep, qualify, narrow, test, or remove |

Apply greater proof requirements to:

- quantified outcomes
- speed, ease, or effort claims
- universal or category-wide claims
- superiority, uniqueness, or competitive comparisons
- trust, security, privacy, compliance, or safety claims
- customer quotations, testimonials, and endorsements

One type of proof cannot establish every claim. Product demonstrations support
behavior. Instrumented use can support adoption patterns. Customer outcome
evidence can support bounded results. Current comparison research can support a
specific competitive claim. Keep these roles distinct.

## Design the message path

Organize messages by the questions the audience must resolve:

1. Is this for someone in my situation?
2. What problem or progress does it address?
3. What is it, and how does it work?
4. Why consider it instead of my current alternative?
5. What proof makes the claim credible?
6. What constraints, effort, or risk should I understand?
7. What is the proportionate next step?

The path is not a fixed page template. Reorder or compress it for the audience,
channel, awareness, and purchase risk while preserving the claim and proof
logic.

Write a message hierarchy:

- one primary promise supported by the position
- two to four supporting ideas with distinct jobs
- product mechanisms that explain credibility
- proof attached to the claim it supports
- constraints and qualifications needed for truth
- a next action proportionate to audience readiness

Do not create several competing headline promises merely to include every
feature.

## Adapt without changing the truth

Different participants may need different emphasis:

- users care about workflow, effort, confidence, and time to value
- buyers care about outcomes, economics, risk, and adoption
- technical evaluators care about behavior, integration, controls, and limits
- operators care about rollout, support, recovery, and ownership

Adapt vocabulary, sequence, detail, and proof. Do not silently change the
product promise or imply incompatible capabilities for different audiences.

Record which statements are:

- stable across channels
- audience-specific
- channel-specific expression
- temporary campaign hypotheses
- prohibited until evidence closes

## Use customer language responsibly

Preserve supplied customer wording only when its source, context, and meaning
are clear. Distinguish:

- verbatim quotation with permission and attribution
- paraphrased account
- recurring observed phrase
- internal interpretation
- proposed copy

Do not convert an internal phrase into “the customer's words,” merge several
people into a fictional quotation, or generalize one account to an entire
segment.

When supplied wording conflicts with observed behavior, preserve the
contradiction and investigate it.

## Address objections with evidence

Classify an objection before answering:

- misunderstanding of the category or product
- missing capability or real constraint
- insufficient proof
- switching or implementation cost
- price or commercial-fit concern
- trust, risk, compliance, or authority concern
- lack of urgency or poor segment fit

Respond with supported information, a discovery question, a qualification, or
an honest limitation. Do not turn every objection into clever rebuttal. A real
product gap or bad fit is not a messaging problem.

## Review message integrity

Check:

- claim and proof remain adjacent enough to understand
- qualifications are visible where the claim is encountered
- examples and demonstrations match the supported product
- comparison basis, date, and scope are explicit
- calls to action do not imply unavailable access or false urgency
- localization preserves meaning and qualification
- channel adaptation has not amplified certainty

Use `web-legal-compliance` for current jurisdiction-specific applicability,
required disclosures, testimonials, endorsements, comparative advertising,
direct marketing, privacy, and consent. This route does not provide legal
conclusions.

## Deliver the message brief

Return:

1. audience, situation, position, and intended next action
2. primary and supporting messages
3. claim-to-proof register
4. customer language with provenance and use limits
5. objections, supported responses, and genuine product gaps
6. required qualifications and prohibited claims
7. channel-stable truths and adaptation boundaries
8. proof-creation and message-test plan

Route finished long-form prose through `nonfiction-writing`, web implementation
through `effective-web`, and LinkedIn execution through
`linkedin-social-selling`.

## Quality Check

- Does each message derive from the approved position?
- Is every consequential claim connected to the right kind of proof?
- Are customer words real, contextualized, and permitted?
- Are constraints preserved rather than hidden?
- Can the audience distinguish product behavior from promised outcome?
- Is the next action honest and proportionate?
