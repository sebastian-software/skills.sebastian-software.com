# Market Learning

Use this route to learn from wins, losses, non-decisions, adoption, failed
adoption, and retained use without allowing hindsight or a convenient sample to
rewrite the original evidence.

## Define the learning decision

State what the review may change:

- target segment
- category context or positioning
- message or proof
- launch scope or readiness
- sales enablement
- channel choice
- product decision handed back to Product Management

Record the original decision, evidence cutoff, assumptions, expected signals,
and decision window. Preserve those artifacts. A later outcome can reveal what
the team learned; it cannot create evidence that was unavailable at the time.

## Build a representative case set

Include cases relevant to the decision:

- wins
- losses
- no-decisions and stalled evaluations
- customers who activated
- customers who failed to reach first value
- customers who reached value but did not repeat it
- retained customers and expanded use
- churn, contraction, or workaround behavior

Segment by the dimensions that may change the explanation:

- customer situation and target fit
- buyer and user roles
- current alternative
- product, package, price, and availability
- sales motion and channel
- geography, language, industry, or risk context
- cohort and time period

Do not let sales convenience, customer friendliness, account size, or a desired
narrative determine the sample. Record exclusions and missing groups.

## Combine evidence without flattening it

Use:

- decision and sales records
- product usage and adoption data
- support, onboarding, implementation, and cancellation evidence
- interviews with participants close to the decision
- observed customer behavior and commitments
- current competitor or market facts when they affected the decision

Separate:

- observed fact
- participant account
- team interpretation
- causal hypothesis
- unresolved contradiction

Do not equate the stated reason in a CRM field with the complete decision.
Price, timing, competition, missing features, trust, authority, implementation
cost, and lack of urgency can overlap.

## Conduct responsible win/loss inquiry

Choose an interviewer who can reduce pressure to protect the relationship or
justify the sale. Make participation voluntary and avoid turning the
conversation into a recovery pitch.

Use `market-research` when the review requires a newly recruited case set,
participant operations, repeated fieldwork, public or licensed source research,
or a cross-source evidence repository. Pass the market decision and evidence
gaps; keep the segment, position, message, proof, launch, enablement, and
channel decision in `product-marketing`.

Ask for a concrete sequence:

1. What changed and started the evaluation?
2. What did the participant do before?
3. Which alternatives entered consideration, and why?
4. Who joined the decision and what mattered to each person?
5. What evidence, event, or concern changed the direction?
6. What was difficult to understand or trust?
7. What happened after the decision?

Prefer past behavior and decision moments over hypothetical improvements. Do
not ask the participant to validate the team's preferred explanation.

Protect privacy, consent, confidentiality, and appropriate retention. Route
current jurisdiction-specific recording, data-use, testimonial, and direct-
marketing requirements through `web-legal-compliance`.

## Trace adoption

Map:

```text
eligible customer -> commitment -> setup -> meaningful start -> first value
                  -> repeated value -> retained outcome
```

For each transition ask:

- Which segment and promise brought the customer here?
- What product and operational conditions were present?
- Where did progress stop, and what evidence explains it?
- Did the message create an expectation the product did not meet?
- Did the product work but onboarding, implementation, or support fail?
- Did the channel acquire people outside the intended segment?
- Did customers reach an outcome that the current instrumentation misses?

Top-of-funnel response can support a channel or message hypothesis. It does not
by itself validate product value, positioning, or retention.

## Test explanations

For each proposed explanation:

1. state the claim at the smallest useful scope
2. list supporting and contradicting evidence
3. identify plausible alternatives
4. check whether the pattern repeats in the relevant segment
5. name what evidence would distinguish the explanations
6. state which decision would change if the claim holds

Avoid universal conclusions from a few cases. Do not treat a competitor mention
as proof of feature, price, or position parity without current verification.
Do not blame sales, marketing, product, or customers before tracing the
complete decision and adoption path.

## Decide which layer changes

Change the smallest supported layer:

- **Product evidence:** correct the factual packet when behavior, constraints,
  quality, or outcomes differ from what was believed.
- **Segment:** change when recurring decision-relevant needs, alternatives,
  buying conditions, or adoption differ materially.
- **Positioning:** change when the selected value or alternative does not
  explain decisions in the target segment.
- **Messaging or proof:** change when the position is relevant but poorly
  understood, insufficiently credible, or expressed beyond its evidence.
- **Launch or enablement:** change when readiness, ownership, demonstration,
  qualification, or handoff fails.
- **Channel:** change when reach, context, economics, or audience quality fails
  while the supported position and product remain useful.
- **Product decision:** route back to `product-management` when the product
  thesis, scope, quality, release, or value path must change.

Do not simultaneously change product, segment, position, message, price, and
channel unless the evidence shows the original decision system is broadly
invalid. Too many changes destroy attribution and learning.

## Update shared artifacts

Return:

1. decision reviewed and original evidence cutoff
2. case-set composition, exclusions, and biases
3. observed patterns, contradictions, and confidence
4. explanations considered and evidence that distinguishes them
5. smallest recommended layer change
6. artifacts and owners affected
7. next test, guardrails, and decision window
8. conditions that would reverse the update

Version the position, message, proof, launch, and enablement artifacts when the
decision changes. Preserve the prior rationale and use `decision-records` when
the repository maintains durable cross-functional decisions.

## Quality Check

- Does the case set include losses, non-decisions, and failed adoption?
- Are stated reasons separated from observed decisions and interpretation?
- Are segment and cohort differences visible?
- Does the recommendation change only the smallest supported layer?
- Are product, price, legal, writing, web, and channel handoffs preserved?
- Can the next evidence contradict the preferred explanation?
