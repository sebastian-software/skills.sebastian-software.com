# Negotiation

Create room for a workable agreement without manufacturing leverage. Separate
facts, interests, options, authority, and commitment state so a productive
conversation does not become an accidental promise.

## Workflow

1. Define the negotiation:
   - parties, roles, decision owners, affected people, and relationship horizon
   - issue, desired outcome, timing, decision process, and known constraints
   - facts already established, claims to verify, and confidential information
   - who may explore, propose, recommend, approve, sign, or communicate terms
2. Load [Preparation and options](references/preparation-and-options.md) to map
   interests, legitimate alternatives, targets, limits, mandate, risks, and
   several dimensions that can be exchanged. Use it before the conversation
   when the stakes or authorization are unclear.
3. Strengthen real alternatives where possible. Never invent another offer,
   approval, deadline, constraint, competitor, or ability to walk away.
4. Prepare two or three coherent packages rather than one demand. Vary terms
   that have different cost or value to the parties, and state the assumptions
   each package depends on.
5. Load [Conversation and proposals](references/conversation-and-proposals.md)
   for agenda setting, information gathering, proposals, conditional exchanges,
   concession control, impasse handling, and written closure.
6. During the conversation:
   - confirm participants, purpose, process, time, and decision authority
   - ask specific questions before solving the other side's position
   - summarize interests, constraints, disagreement, and unknowns for correction
   - label options as exploratory, proposals as conditional, and commitments as
     authorized
   - exchange terms deliberately rather than conceding one issue at a time
7. Pause when a new term exceeds the mandate, relies on unverified facts,
   materially changes risk, or requires an absent specialist. Record the open
   question and obtain approval instead of improvising consent.
8. Load [Professional contexts](references/professional-contexts.md) for
   commercial terms, product scope, delivery commitments, service levels,
   migrations, vendor discussions, or cross-team agreements.
9. Before closing, read back every material term, owner, dependency, date,
   condition, exception, and unresolved point. Distinguish an agreement in
   principle from an approved or signed agreement.
10. Send a written summary promptly. Ask the other party to correct it, route
    required reviews, and store the authorized result in the system that owns
    the contract, product commitment, technical decision, or work.
11. Review the outcome: what was learned, which assumptions changed, what was
    conceded or protected, whether execution is on track, and when the parties
    will revisit the agreement.

## Operating Rules

- Negotiate from interests and constraints, not from pressure alone. A stated
  position is the start of inquiry, not proof of the underlying need.
- Treat alternatives as actions available without this agreement. Label their
  cost, timing, probability, dependencies, and owner honestly.
- Prepare a target, acceptable range, stop or escalation boundary, and approval
  path. Do not collapse ambition, acceptability, and authority into one number.
- Create value by finding terms the parties value differently: scope, timing,
  volume, commitment length, payment timing, service, risk allocation,
  reversibility, reference rights, or implementation support.
- Use objective standards where they are relevant and inspectable. Do not use a
  benchmark, policy, precedent, or technical limit as false authority.
- Give reasons without disclosing confidential information or pretending a
  preference is an immutable constraint.
- Make concessions conditional, specific, and recorded. Do not concede against
  yourself, imply reciprocity that was not agreed, or reopen settled terms
  casually.
- Protect the other party's ability to understand and choose. Do not use false
  urgency, hidden terms, impersonation, harassment, discrimination, or
  intentional ambiguity.
- Preserve relationships without buying harmony through unauthorized promises.
  A respectful no, pause, or escalation is often safer than a vague yes.
- Never describe exploratory language, a meeting note, or an agreement in
  principle as a final authorization.

## Default Deliverable

For a consequential negotiation, return:

1. Negotiation scope, parties, decision process, and relationship context
2. Facts, unknowns, interests, constraints, and information plan
3. Real alternatives, target, acceptable range, limits, and mandate
4. Two or three multi-term packages with rationale and risk
5. Conversation plan, questions, proposal sequence, and concession log
6. Authority and specialist-review checkpoints
7. Written outcome with agreed, conditional, open, and rejected terms
8. Execution owners, dates, dependencies, and review trigger

## Routing Boundaries

- Use `pricing-and-packaging` to research or decide value metrics, price levels,
  packages, discounts, trials, or migration policy. Use this skill to negotiate
  authorized commercial terms within that decision.
- Use `product-management` to decide product direction, customer promise,
  roadmap, scope, priority, or release tradeoffs before representing them as
  negotiable commitments.
- Use `software-architecture` to establish technical feasibility, system risk,
  operational constraints, and credible migration options before trading them.
- Use `decision-records` for durable internal product or technical choices when
  the repository uses shared decision records; it does not replace a contract
  or signed agreement.
- Crisis, hostage, self-harm, abuse, coercive-control, and public-safety
  negotiations require trained responders and remain outside this skill.
- Legal representation, jurisdiction-specific contract interpretation,
  employment-law advice, and signing authority remain outside this skill and
  require qualified, authorized specialists.
