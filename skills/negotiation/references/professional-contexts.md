# Professional Contexts

Load only the section that matches the negotiation. The core workflow remains
the same; each context adds evidence, authority, and execution checks.

## Commercial Terms

For customer, vendor, procurement, renewal, or partnership terms, prepare:

- offer, target customer, use case, and approved commercial policy
- price, package, volume, payment timing, term, renewal, and cancellation
- implementation, support, service levels, and change process
- data, security, confidentiality, publicity, and reference implications
- cost-to-serve, operational exceptions, and approval owners
- contract owner, legal-review triggers, and signing authority

Use `pricing-and-packaging` to research or decide price, value metric, packages,
discount policy, trial, or existing-customer migration. Negotiation can exchange
only terms within that approved range and mandate.

Do not promise a custom commercial exception before billing, entitlement,
support, product, finance, and contract owners can represent it consistently.

## Product Scope and Roadmap

For customer requests, stakeholder commitments, roadmap dependencies, or
cross-team scope:

- name the user and business outcome beneath the requested feature or date
- distinguish discovery, candidate scope, planned work, forecast, commitment,
  and released capability
- surface quality bar, non-goals, dependencies, opportunity cost, and evidence
- trade scope, sequence, learning steps, or decision dates instead of hiding
  uncertainty behind a delivery promise
- identify the product owner and how a change enters the shared plan

Use `product-management` to decide product direction, priority, scope, release,
or quality. Negotiation does not manufacture roadmap authority or turn one
customer's request into a product strategy.

When a commercial package depends on future product work, keep the commercial
condition and product approval explicitly linked.

## Technical Commitments

For integrations, migrations, service levels, reliability, security work, data
movement, or technical dependencies, establish:

- current architecture and observed behavior
- target state, scope, interfaces, data, and ownership boundary
- capacity, skills, sequence, lead time, and external dependencies
- failure modes, rollback, recovery, support, and monitoring
- exact service-level definitions and measurement source
- assumptions that could invalidate the date or guarantee
- technical owner, operational owner, and approval path

Use `software-architecture` to assess feasibility, system risk, boundaries, and
migration options. A negotiator may translate verified tradeoffs but must not
invent certainty for leverage.

Prefer conditional milestones, discovery checkpoints, ranges, or pilots when
the evidence cannot support a hard date or absolute guarantee.

## Cross-Team Agreements

For internal dependencies and shared work:

- define the common outcome and affected customers
- name requested capacity, sequence, interface, and service expectation
- make opportunity cost and competing commitments visible
- identify which manager or product owner can allocate the work
- distinguish cooperation from an approved priority change
- record escalation and re-planning conditions

Do not use hierarchy, urgency, or public pressure to manufacture consent. Do not
route around the accountable owner because another team member was easier to
persuade.

## Written Outcome by Context

Ensure the record includes:

| Context | Minimum execution detail |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial | exact terms, billing and renewal behavior, approvals, contract owner |
| Product | outcome, scope state, priority owner, dependencies, quality, review point |
| Technical | interfaces, assumptions, milestones, service definitions, rollback, owners |
| Cross-team | capacity, sequence, decision owner, dependencies, escalation trigger |

State what is not agreed. A concise list of exclusions prevents a broad phrase
such as “support the migration” from becoming an unlimited obligation.

## Specialist Boundaries

- Route legal interpretation, contract drafting, regulated terms, employment
  law, tax, accounting, and signing authority to qualified authorized owners.
- Route security, privacy, compliance, safety, and production-operability
  reviews to the applicable project specialists.
- Stop the negotiation workflow when coercion, abuse, crisis, hostage, self-harm,
  or public-safety risk appears; use trained response and safety channels.
