## Open the Conversation

Confirm:

- participants and roles
- purpose, agenda, available time, and next decision point
- what can be decided now and which approvals remain
- any shared facts or documents
- whether notes may be taken and who will send the summary

Use a calm, specific opening. Do not create urgency, authority, or agreement by
implication.

## Gather Information Before Solving

Ask open questions to surface the situation, then specific questions to test
constraints and priorities. Useful sequence:

1. What outcome are you trying to protect or create?
2. Why is that important now?
3. What makes the current proposal difficult?
4. Which terms are essential, and which are flexible?
5. Who else must approve or implement the result?
6. What would a workable next step look like?

Listen for differences between the stated position and the underlying need.
Summarize in neutral language and ask for correction:

```text
I heard that timing and implementation risk matter more than the nominal term,
and that final approval sits with finance. What did I miss?
```

Do not claim agreement because the other party did not object.

## Make Clear Proposals

State a proposal as a complete conditional package:

```text
If [specific term or action], then we can [specific term or action], subject to
[named approval, verification, or condition], by [date or decision point].
```

Include scope, timing, dependencies, duration, exceptions, and approval state.
Explain the legitimate reason for a term without turning a preference into an
immutable constraint.

Label commitment state:

- **Exploratory:** an option offered to learn, not a promise.
- **Proposed:** a conditional package within or subject to a mandate.
- **Agreement in principle:** direction accepted, material reviews or details
  still open.
- **Authorized:** approved by the required owner within their authority.
- **Signed or executed:** completed through the system and parties that own the
  binding agreement.

Never skip states merely to create momentum.

## Control Concessions

Maintain a live log:

| Term | Requested by | Cost or risk | Value to them | Condition | Authority | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Before changing a term:

- restate the reason for the request
- check whether another dimension solves the interest at lower cost
- make the exchange conditional and specific
- verify authorization
- update the package and cumulative impact

Avoid unreciprocated successive concessions. Do not imply that a courtesy,
pause, or exploratory statement earned a material concession.

## Respond to Pressure and Tactics

When faced with a deadline, benchmark, policy, competing offer, or technical
claim:

- ask for the relevant details that can be shared
- verify facts that materially change the decision
- compare the claim with the parties' interests and real alternatives
- name the consequence of acting without verification
- pause or propose a bounded next step

Do not retaliate with deception. A truthful response can be firm:

```text
I cannot approve that term in this meeting. I can document the package and get
an answer from the owner by Thursday.
```

If behavior becomes harassing, discriminatory, coercive, or unsafe, stop the
ordinary negotiation and use the organization's escalation and safety process.

## Work Through Impasse

Diagnose whether the impasse comes from:

- missing information
- different forecasts or standards
- authority absent from the room
- one overloaded issue
- incompatible limits
- implementation or trust risk
- timing rather than substance

Then choose a proportionate move: verify a fact, introduce the owner, change
sequence, split a reversible pilot from a later decision, adjust another term,
take a break, or conclude that no agreement is preferable.

Do not make a concession solely because silence or delay feels uncomfortable.

## Close and Confirm

Read back:

- every agreed term and its status
- every condition, dependency, exception, and date
- owners and required approvals
- unresolved and rejected terms
- implementation start condition
- next meeting or expiration point

Send a written summary promptly. Use explicit sections:

1. Authorized or signed terms
2. Agreement in principle
3. Conditional proposals
4. Open questions and required reviews
5. Rejected or withdrawn terms
6. Owners, dates, and next actions

Ask recipients to correct inaccuracies. Store final agreements in the
contract, product, technical, procurement, or work system that owns them; a
chat message or meeting note is not automatically binding authorization.
