# Route: Consultant Profile — Thesis, Prominence, and Structure

Help a buyer, recruiter, or staffing lead see what the consultant does, what
proves it, and which work they should be hired for next.

## Guidance by need

- [Profile principles](profile-principles.md): prominence, structure, country
  and channel conventions, writing formulas, and audit criteria.
- [Narrative arc](narrative-arc.md): recurring themes and connections between
  projects, when a career history needs a through-line.
- [Profile evidence](route-profile-evidence.md): incomplete or conflicting
  sources, interviews, recommendations, and named provider fields.
- [Profile voice](route-profile-voice.md): personality, boundaries, tone, or
  localization.

Load the reference that addresses the request. A headline edit or one project
entry does not require a complete profile audit, interview, or channel strategy.

## Profile decisions

1. Use the target reader, buying situation, channel, market, language, and
   existing editorial direction supplied in context. Infer low-risk gaps;
   ask when the missing choice changes fit or a consequential claim.
2. Identify the professional thesis from project evidence: specialty, customer
   context, and supportable value. For a full profile, map roles, clients,
   industry, scale, responsibilities, deliverables, and outcomes to that thesis.
3. Give high-signal projects prominence by relevance rather than chronology
   alone. Older work may be the strongest proof. Use the professional arc when
   connections between projects are supported and help the reader assess fit.
4. Write project proof as Outcome → Action → Scope when impact is known, or
   Challenge → Role → Deliverable when outcomes are unavailable. Preserve
   ownership, uncertainty, and material constraints.
5. Put the strongest thesis and proof early. Include other sections only when
   they help this reader assess fit or satisfy the requested format.

## Evidence boundaries

Never invent metrics, client claims, leadership scope, outcomes, or fit scores.
Map an opportunity's explicit requirements to supplied evidence and leave gaps
unresolved. Qualitative context must also be factual; a missing metric does
not justify calling work “enterprise-scale” without support.

Keep external recommendations attributed. Preserve the author, collaboration
context, historical role/company, source, date, original meaning, reuse
restrictions, and translation or omission labels. Verify restricted client
names, sensitive metrics, private quotations, and confidential outcomes before
publication; continue with supported material while gaps remain.

Technical precision does not require a controlled-language profile. Preserve
verified terms and claims in natural buyer-facing prose; use the documentation
owner for an actual controlled-language contract.

## Deliverable

Return the requested rewrite or prioritized audit first. For an audit, explain
the consequential thesis, prominence, evidence, or structure problems with
concrete fixes. Include sample rewrites when useful. For a full profile,
produce the sections the target channel needs. Add an interview, quote map,
provider field map, tone variants, or social variants only when in scope or
necessary to resolve a material gap.
