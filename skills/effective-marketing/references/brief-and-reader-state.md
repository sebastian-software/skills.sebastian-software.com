# Brief and Reader State

Marketing copy starts with a decision-ready writing brief, not with a headline
formula. Build only the detail needed to keep the draft relevant, supportable,
and reviewable.

## Build the minimum brief

Capture:

| Field | What must be stable enough to write |
| --- | --- |
| Artifact | Page, section, campaign, email, launch asset, or other deliverable |
| Reader | Decision-relevant situation, role, and eligibility |
| Entry | Source, promise already seen, prior knowledge, and immediate context |
| Progress | What the reader is trying to change or accomplish |
| Alternative | Current behavior, incumbent, substitute, delay, or no action |
| Offer | What is available, to whom, under which material conditions |
| Mechanism | How the offer plausibly creates the promised progress |
| Position | Relevant difference and category context already approved |
| Claims | Exact claims that may be made and qualifications that keep them true |
| Proof | Evidence for each consequential claim and where it may be used |
| Friction | Questions, effort, switching cost, uncertainty, and objections |
| Action | Primary next step, commitment level, and what happens afterward |
| Voice | Useful vocabulary, tone boundaries, and approved technical terms |
| Exclusions | Audiences, promises, frames, or tactics that must not appear |

Do not ask the user to repeat information already present in supplied product,
market, pricing, brand, or campaign material. Extract it, identify
contradictions, and ask only for gaps that could materially change the copy.

## Keep evidence types distinct

Build a compact evidence register:

```text
claim or language | source | observed or interpreted | context | permission
| material limit | usable in this artifact?
```

- Verified product behavior can establish what the product does under known
  conditions.
- Customer language can establish how a particular person described a
  situation. It does not establish prevalence.
- A case or testimonial can establish one attributed experience. It does not
  establish a typical or guaranteed outcome.
- Research can support a general claim only within its population, method,
  period, and uncertainty.
- A stakeholder preference can guide voice or emphasis. It cannot make an
  unsupported claim true.

When proof is missing, choose one:

1. make a narrower factual claim;
2. describe the mechanism or capability instead of the outcome;
3. label the statement as an aspiration or hypothesis;
4. insert a clearly marked evidence placeholder for internal review;
5. route the evidence gap to its owner and omit the claim.

## Model the reader's decision state

Do not reduce the reader to demographics or a fixed awareness ladder. Record
the state that changes what must be explained:

- Which situation do they already recognize?
- Which outcome do they care about now?
- What have they tried, compared, delayed, or normalized?
- What do they already know about the problem, category, product, and offer?
- Which belief must become credible before the next step feels reasonable?
- Which practical, financial, social, technical, or identity risk matters?
- What commitment can they responsibly make at this moment?

The same person may arrive in different states through an ad, recommendation,
search result, sales conversation, product screen, or renewal notice. Match the
opening, detail, proof, and call to action to that entry state.

## Preserve message match

For every incoming promise, record:

```text
source promise -> expected destination answer -> supporting proof -> next step
```

The destination should recognize the same situation and deliver the promised
answer quickly. Do not lure a reader with one claim and switch to a broader or
more demanding offer after arrival.

## Resolve multiple audiences

Avoid one paragraph that tries to address everyone. Choose among:

- one primary reader and clear qualification;
- distinct entry paths for materially different situations;
- a shared top-level promise followed by audience-specific proof and routes;
- separate artifacts when audiences require different claims, objections, or
  commitments.

If no audience can be prioritized, return the tradeoff to the positioning and messaging routes.
Writing cannot repair an unresolved segment or position by becoming vague.

## Brief quality check

- Can every important statement be traced to an approved source?
- Does the reader description change the copy, or is it decorative?
- Is the current alternative explicit, including delay or no action?
- Is the mechanism clear enough to connect capability and value?
- Are proof and qualifications available for the strongest claims?
- Does the primary action match the reader's likely readiness and risk?
- Are traffic promises, page promises, and follow-through consistent?
- Are unresolved choices assigned to the owner who can decide them?
