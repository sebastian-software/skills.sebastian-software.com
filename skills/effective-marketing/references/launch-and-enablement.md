# Launch and Enablement

Use this route to coordinate a market-facing change, prepare customer-facing
teams, and define controlled exposure and learning. Treat launch as a decision
system, not as a universal calendar or one-day event.

## Define the launch decision

State:

- what is changing and for which eligible audience
- product, package, geography, language, channel, and availability scope
- current evidence and riskiest unsupported assumption
- accountable launch owner and decision authority
- fixed commitments versus adjustable plans
- exposure, reversibility, and consequences of failure
- desired customer and business outcome

Do not begin with a date, asset list, or channel schedule when eligibility,
product behavior, support, commercial terms, or authority remain unresolved.

## Establish readiness

Build one launch register with owners and status.

### Product readiness

- supported behavior, states, limits, and known issues
- target customer's complete path to first and repeated value
- quality, accessibility, reliability, privacy, security, and recovery gates
- eligibility, availability, rollout control, and rollback condition
- demonstration and documentation behavior matching production

Route unresolved product evidence, scope, quality, or release decisions to
`effective-product`.

### Market readiness

- selected segment, situation, category, and alternatives
- approved position and message hierarchy
- claim-to-proof register and material proof gaps
- current competitor facts and comparison boundaries
- language, localization, and audience comprehension

### Commercial readiness

- approved price, package, entitlement, discount, trial, and migration facts
- purchase, billing, cancellation, renewal, and exception behavior
- sales qualification and disqualification
- customer commitments the organization can authorize and fulfill

Route unresolved pricing or packaging decisions to `effective-product`.

### Operational readiness

- support owner, escalation path, and response expectations
- onboarding, implementation, training, and customer-success capacity
- analytics definitions, data quality, and dashboard ownership
- incident, complaint, refund, and rollback procedures
- legal and compliance review appropriate to the market and claims

Do not average blocking readiness gaps into an overall reassuring score. Mark
the launch `ready`, `conditionally ready`, `not ready`, or `insufficient
evidence` for the declared exposure.

## Choose the Distribution Motion from Economics and Trust

Start with the actual constraint, not a universal channel sequence. Establish:

- price, gross margin, retention, payback boundary, and confidence in each;
- buyer effort, required trust, implementation risk, and human-touch needs;
- full acquisition, sales, onboarding, support, and delivery cost and capacity;
- existing audience, relationships, partners, product surfaces, and other
  credible access advantages; and
- downstream conversion, adoption, retention, and operational signals.

Compare only plausible motions: high-touch sales, product-led acquisition,
demand capture, partners, founder trust, content, paid distribution, or public
relations. Name the function of each: founder-led work can transfer trust and
return direct evidence but creates concentration risk; product content can
support independent understanding and discovery; paid channels can capture
demand or amplify a proven response but do not manufacture value; public
relations can concentrate attention around real news, proof, or a customer
moment. Do not copy another company's sequence, staffing ratio, or spend split.
Test the smallest credible motion and scale only when conversion, retention,
capacity, and economics support it.

## Diagnose Whether Building Is Avoiding Distribution

When feature work dominates, identify the current constraint across readiness,
reach, comprehension, evaluation, commitment, activation, retention, capacity,
and economics. Define the minimum route to market: audience and situation,
current alternative and promise, explanation and action, a way to reach people
and capture response, owners, and decision evidence.

Compare the marginal value of more engineering with distribution, activation,
and market learning. Distinguish a true product gap from feature accumulation;
route the former to the relevant `effective-product` route, otherwise prioritize
the missing market path. State the observable imbalance without diagnosing
leadership motives. Compare headcount, budget, and attention with the evidenced
constraint, not with a copied company ratio.

## Turn Services into a Scaling System

Map the steps whose effort grows roughly with each customer. For every step,
choose deliberately whether to preserve it as valuable human service,
standardize it, automate it, productize it, deliver it through partners, or
remove it. Protect the customer knowledge and relationships the service
produces. Choose the scaling mechanism from evidence; do not assume that
building an audience, adding software, or removing people is always the answer.

## Select the launch scope and sequence

Choose the smallest exposure that can test the intended position and value
without misleading the market or creating uncontrolled harm. Options include:

- internal or invited demonstration
- named-customer pilot
- segment- or region-limited availability
- waitlist with an explicit promise
- staged customer migration
- broad availability after critical gates pass

For each stage define:

1. eligible audience and entry condition
2. message, offer, and supported promise
3. operational capacity and owner
4. primary signal and trust or business guardrails
5. learning window
6. expand, hold, narrow, or stop rule

Do not call a broad irreversible rollout an experiment merely because metrics
exist. Do not imply product availability, scarcity, or deadlines that are not
real.

## Prepare sales enablement

Equip sales and customer-facing teams to discover fit and communicate supported
value, not merely to repeat slogans.

Provide:

- target situation, qualification and disqualification signals
- discovery questions tied to customer progress and alternatives
- concise category and positioning explanation
- value narrative connected to product mechanism
- approved claims, proof, constraints, and prohibited claims
- demonstration path using supported product behavior
- common objections classified by misunderstanding, proof gap, product gap,
  switching cost, commercial fit, or risk
- competitive context with source, date, comparison basis, and unknowns
- pricing and package facts from the commercial owner
- authorized next steps, commitments, and escalation paths
- a structured way to return objections, losses, and adoption evidence

Treat battlecards, scripts, and objection responses as aids to judgment. Never
invent competitor weaknesses, fake customer outcomes, guaranteed savings, or
commitments outside the seller's authority.

## Coordinate launch artifacts

Create only the artifacts the launch path requires. Assign one owner and source
of truth for:

- internal launch brief
- customer-facing message and proof brief
- web, product, email, social, event, partner, or sales handoffs
- demonstration, FAQ, and enablement material
- availability, support, legal, and escalation facts
- measurement definitions and decision review

Preserve separation:

- Product Marketing owns the approved audience, position, message, proof, and
  launch coordination.
- The channel owner adapts and operates the channel.
- Product and engineering own actual behavior and release controls.
- Commercial owners approve price, package, and commitments.
- Legal specialists determine applicable obligations and acceptable wording.

Do not let duplicated launch documents drift. Record unresolved facts as
placeholders with owners rather than filling them with plausible copy.

## Measure the whole launch path

Connect attention to downstream value:

```text
eligible exposure -> qualified response -> evaluation -> commitment
                  -> first value -> repeated value -> retained outcome
```

Choose measures for the decision:

- audience reach and qualification
- message comprehension and response
- evaluation, sales progression, and reason for non-decision
- activation, first value, and time to value
- repeated use, retained outcome, and expansion
- support, trust, complaint, refund, and operational load
- acquisition and delivery economics

Do not declare success from impressions, traffic, sign-ups, press, or internal
enthusiasm alone. Segment results by eligibility, audience, offer, channel, and
product exposure when those dimensions affect the claim.

## Deliver the launch plan

Return:

1. launch decision, exposure, audience, and accountable owner
2. readiness register with blocking gaps and owners
3. staged sequence with entry, exit, rollback, and learning conditions
4. message, claim, proof, and qualification handoffs
5. sales enablement packet and authority boundaries
6. channel and operational owners
7. measurement path, guardrails, and review date
8. expand, hold, narrow, stop, or escalate rules

## Quality Check

- Does the proposed exposure match readiness and reversibility?
- Can every customer-facing team distinguish fact, claim, assumption, and limit?
- Does enablement help discover fit rather than force a pitch?
- Are pricing, product, legal, and channel decisions owned by the right people?
- Does measurement reach adoption and retained value?
- Are rollback and learning decisions explicit before launch?
