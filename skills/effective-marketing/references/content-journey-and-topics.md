# Content Journey and Topic Bank

Connect a content program to a customer and an offer. Qualified conversations
and decisions matter more than raw reach.

## Give each piece one primary job

A content system helps the right people discover a service, understand their
problem, build trust, and start a conversation. One post does not have to do
every job. Choose its primary outcome before drafting:

1. Reach relevant people who do not yet know the provider.
2. Answer a specific search question.
3. Prompt self-identification through a relevant reply.
4. Generate a direct question or message.
5. Help an already interested person decide to book a call.

Decide whether a topic serves narrow search intent or broader discovery. A
search topic can name the role and problem precisely. A broader topic needs an
accessible opening without losing the intended buyer. Base the balance on the
offer and current audience, not a fixed posting ratio.

## Map the buyer's journey

| Stage | Audience question | Useful content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | “Does this affect me?” | Symptoms, search questions, short examples |
| Understanding | “Why does this happen, and whom should I trust?” | Diagnosis, mistaken assumptions, mechanism, deeper explanations, cases |
| Decision | “Is this right for my situation?” | Preconditions, process, trade-offs, results, objections, clear contact option |

A new channel often needs more discoverability. An established audience may need
more decision-stage material. Avoid a feed that only seeks attention or only
pushes calls.

## Decision-stage content

Some pieces should directly support an inquiry: name a known problem, correct a
misconception, explain a result, then invite a suitable question or appointment.
Match the ask to the audience's readiness. Do not manufacture fear or imply that
every viewer needs the service. Review current platform rules before relying on
comment prompts, automated direct messages, or particular ad formats.

## Build a topic bank

Mine customer conversations for symptoms, unsuccessful attempts, decision
questions, objections, alternatives, and observed outcomes. For each segment,
keep five topic fields: recognizable symptoms, expensive mistakes, frequent
questions, solution comparisons, and genuine proof. Convert them into questions
such as “How can [role] tell whether [symptom] comes from [cause]?” or “What
must be true for [solution] to work?”

Include narrow, purchase-relevant subjects even if they attract fewer views. B2B
content can focus tightly on role, industry, systems, and economic consequences;
a small audience can be enough. Consumer content may need an accessible everyday
entry point but should still identify the intended person before asking for
action.

## Editorial record

```text
Target segment and problem:
Journey stage and primary content goal:
Working title or search question:
Core insight:
Example or approved proof:
Channel and format:
Next action:
Production status and publication date:
Relevant replies, qualified conversations, and learning:
```

## Learn from outcomes

Track views or reach, profile visits, replies, messages, qualified
conversations, and sales where attribution is credible. Buyers often have
several touchpoints; avoid assigning every sale to a last click. Low reach with
strong B2B conversations may be a good outcome. Broad reach without suitable
prospects suggests weak audience filtering. Many reactions without conversations
suggest an unclear next step or an entertainment-only topic. Repeated objections
become future explanatory content. Compare trends over multiple publications and
against sales data.
