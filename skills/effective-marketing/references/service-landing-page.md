# Plan a Landing Page

## One page, one primary action

The first screen should make the intended customer, problem or desired outcome,
and next step obvious. Then explain the mechanism, evidence, prerequisites,
scope, and common objections. Repeat the same call to action at natural decision
points. Competing goals make the page harder to use.

## Build brief

```text
Offer and best-fit customer:
Visitor source and promise made there:
One problem this page must address:
Primary conversion event:
Explanation needed before an inquiry:
Approved cases, quotes, and assets:
Common objections:
Market-specific disclosures and technical constraints:
```

If the audience or offer is unclear, resolve that first. A template speeds
layout but cannot fix a contradictory promise.

## Argument-led wireframe

1. **Opening:** State the problem or outcome, intended customer, and one action.
   Keep the ad or post promise consistent with the headline.
2. **Recognition:** Show situations and symptoms a suitable customer can
   identify.
3. **Diagnosis:** Explain why common attempts may fall short and what the
   evidence suggests instead.
4. **Approach:** Describe three to five steps, their purpose, and required
   customer participation.
5. **Proof:** Use a real case, work sample, or checkable quality standard. Give
   numbers a period and context.
6. **Fit:** Show who benefits, boundaries, effort, and conditions.
7. **Action:** Present a form or booking step, explain what happens next, and
   include appropriate privacy information.

A short contact page can combine sections. A complex service may need more
explanation and trust. Length should follow the difficulty of the buyer's
decision, not a universal formula. Do not guarantee an outcome the team cannot
support.

## Copy and visual review

Highlight sentences that praise the provider without helping a customer decide.
Replace them with a concrete service, result, method, or proof. After two
screens, can a visitor tell why they are here and what happens next? Label
buttons by the real action, such as “Request a consultation” rather than
“Start.” Keep color, type, and images consistent, and check readability and form
use on a phone.

The page must continue the same message as the ad, post, or mail that sent the
visitor. Verify domain, secure connection, links, media, and loading behavior
before release. For design or development handoff, specify section order, final
copy, assets, links, responsive behavior, form data, and acceptance criteria.
Recheck calls to action, forms, and tracking after major copy or layout changes.
