# Local Lead Campaigns

## Test whether local targeting makes sense

A local campaign needs a real geographic connection: service area, physical
location, local case, or place-specific problem. Confirm there are enough
suitable buyers and the business can serve them. Combining a narrow region with
a narrow B2B industry may leave too little audience. Do not use a location claim
that is not true.

## Campaign brief

```text
Market and serviceable area:
Reachable customer segment and problem:
Desired outcome and supportable proof:
Offer and next action:
One primary result event:
Budget range and lead-handling capacity:
Permitted contact path and responsible owner:
Campaign dates and review point:
```

Define a qualified inquiry before launch. Connect campaign source to the CRM so
meetings and sales can be associated with the acquisition path. Ensure someone
can respond promptly to new inquiries. Set budget and geographic range from
market size, delivery capacity, and observed data rather than borrowing an old
example.

## Create distinct openings

Draft at least three meaningful angles: a concrete local problem, a genuine
local example, and a clearly relevant service in the area. Keep the next action
consistent across them. Make the geographic relevance visible in the image or
first seconds, first line, and headline when that is important to the audience.

Ad structure: **customer and situation → problem or desired outcome → credible
approach → specific evidence → clear action**. Evidence may be an approved
customer example, demonstration, or verifiable description of the work. Avoid
false scarcity, unsupported superlatives, or a form that promises something
different from the destination page.

Review each creative: Can a suitable person identify relevance quickly? Is the
result understandable? Is there a reason to trust this provider? Is the action
obvious? Test a different opening or proof point, not only a color change.

## Choose the contact path

| Path | Fits when | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform form | Service is easy to explain and response speed matters | Fit questions, routing, response time |
| Landing page | Buyer needs explanation, examples, or qualification | Ad-message continuity, loading, mobile use, event |
| Chat | Questions are the main barrier and staff can reply promptly | Owner, conversation guide, CRM handoff |

A form should ask only what the next step requires. If a sales team will call,
it needs to know which ad and promise the prospect saw.

## Prelaunch acceptance

Check creative, copy, location, placement, form, thank-you page, event, CRM
handoff, current platform policies, and local requirements. Run one full journey
from ad click to assigned CRM task. Record variants, audience, budget, dates,
and capacity. Launch only when a test contact arrives with the correct source,
contact method, and owner.
