# Direct Mail, Video Follow-up, and Events

## Choose a channel deliberately

Compare segment precision, preparation time, variable cost, selling skills
required, and local contact rules. Begin with a channel the team can run and
measure reliably before adding several at once.

## Physical mail with phone follow-up

Physical mail can work for a well-researched segment when the potential value
justifies design, printing, postage, and follow-up. Map the recipient's
decision: open the envelope, recognize relevance, understand the problem, trust
the argument, and choose an action. A report or letter can include a precise
title, observed market situation, diagnosis of a common mistake, solution
principle, brief supported cases, and a clear contact route. Test whether an
informative report or a personal letter fits the actual audience.

Before printing, verify recipient and address, every claim, consistency with the
website, QR code, URL, phone number, and the callback process. Inspect a
physical sample. Calculate total unit cost including research, creative work,
printing, postage, and sales time. Log send and follow-up dates in the CRM. In
the call, refer honestly to the piece sent and assess need; do not wait only for
spontaneous replies.

## Call → video → callback

1. **Initial call:** Confirm a relevant problem and whether the prospect wants a
   specific short video. The goal is fit and interest, not a complete pitch.
2. **Video email:** Send to a confirmed address after the person has expressed
   interest and the contact is permitted. Refer briefly to the conversation,
   provide one link, and identify one next step.
3. **Callback:** Discuss the content, answer questions, and assess fit. Opening
   a link is not evidence of buying intent.

An approximately three- to four-minute video can cover problem, alternative
perspective, high-level method, one or two supported cases, and a contact
option. A simple page with the video and one clear action may be enough. Revise
it based on questions prospects actually ask. Record each of the three steps
separately so drop-offs are visible.

## Event roles

| Role | Operating focus |
| --- | --- |
| Attend alone | Prioritize good conversations and record relevant context |
| Attend with a team | Assign conversation, note-taking, and follow-up roles |
| Speak | Deliver a useful session, then make questions and contact options clear |
| Exhibit with a team | Test handoffs, materials, calendar, and CRM capture in advance |

Select events by actual presence of the target audience and total cost. Open
naturally about the attendee's situation. Record only necessary information with
a clear contact purpose. Agree on a next step, then follow up promptly with
reference to the meeting. Judge the event by qualified conversations and later
opportunities relative to time, travel, and fees, not business cards collected.
