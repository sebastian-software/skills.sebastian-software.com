# Retargeting

## Define the audience and message

Retargeting uses a permitted prior signal, such as a site visit or content
interaction. Specify which event qualifies, a sensible lookback period, and who
must be excluded. A follow-up ad should address an unresolved question,
objection, or next step rather than repeating the cold-audience message. These
are hypotheses about an audience, not claims about a particular person's
thoughts.

| Prior signal | Likely question to address | Possible message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General site visit | Is this relevant? | Who the service is for and what it solves |
| Service-page visit | How does working together happen? | Process, outcome, and customer contribution |
| Pricing or FAQ visit | What is included for the cost? | Scope, comparison criteria, and examples |
| Meaningful content engagement | Can this provider do the work? | Demonstration, case, or specific method |
| Form started but not completed | What happens if I inquire? | Low-friction step and treatment of submitted details |

Avoid messages that reveal sensitive inferences about individuals.

## Technical sequence

1. Identify account owners and access.
2. Plan site tags or platform signals.
3. Check the applicable tracking and privacy rules for the target market.
4. Define meaningful events, especially valid inquiry or confirmed booking.
5. Verify events through controlled trial actions.
6. Build audiences and exclusions.
7. Align creative, destination, and funnel stage.
8. Launch and compare actual inquiries with platform reports.

Platform screens and capabilities change. Use current official documentation
when changing settings, and review applicable ad policies for claims, imagery,
audience, and destination.

## Audience record

For each audience, record signal source, legal or platform conditions, lookback
period, minimum viable size, exclusions, message, destination, and success
measure. Exclude current customers, completed inquiries, or booked appointments
when the message no longer fits. Staged exclusions can prevent contradictory ads
appearing to the same person.

Before launch, verify with a trial user that the event fires only in the
permitted circumstances, audience size changes plausibly, exclusions work, ad
and page align, and an inquiry reaches the CRM with attribution.

## Small audiences and performance

When volume is low, combine related signals, extend a defensible lookback
window, or use broader content. Widen only while the same message remains
relevant. Compare reach, frequency, cost, qualified contacts, and later sales.
Small samples fluctuate; avoid changing a campaign after one weak day. Repeated
exposure without added results may justify changing creative, exclusions, or
frequency.
