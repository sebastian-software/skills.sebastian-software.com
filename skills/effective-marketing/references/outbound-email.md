# Direct Email Outreach

## Check the market and contact basis

Before sending, identify the recipient's location, business or consumer status,
source of the address, relationship, message purpose, and applicable marketing
rules. Requirements differ by country and sometimes by region. Check current
official guidance for sender identification, consent or other contact basis,
required disclosures, opt-out, and data handling. Do not assume that a B2B
address or a publicly listed address is universally available for marketing.

## Choose a sequence

**Find the responsible person:** When the role is genuinely unclear in a larger
organization, a first message can ask who owns a named business problem. Review
replies manually and continue only when there is a real fit. Do not mask a sales
pitch behind an unrelated administrative question.

**Write to a known buyer:** When the person and relevance are known, open with a
verified observation or specific problem. Ask a small question rather than
sending a long service overview. A later message should add a new idea or proof,
not merely repeat the first.

## First message structure

Use a truthful subject, a verified reason for contact, a concise problem or
hypothesis, one sentence on the approach, and one easy question. Avoid
fabricated personalization, unsupported results, unexplained attachments, and
long self-introductions. The message should be legible on a phone.

```text
Subject: [specific topic in the buyer's language]

Hi [name],
I noticed [verified observation]. That may make [specific problem]
relevant for [company]. How are you handling [task] today?

I ask because [one sentence on relevant experience or method].
If this is timely, I could [small next step].
```

For a routing inquiry: “Who owns [clearly named task] on your team? I have a
brief, specific question about [topic] for the right person.” When referred,
disclose the context accurately.

## Operate the sequence

Prepare an authenticated sending domain, working reply address, verified small
sample, manual reply handling, meeting handoff, and CRM statuses. Distinguish
delivery failure, refusal, question, referral, and genuine interest. Define four
response paths before sending: interested, asks a question, wrong person, and
decline. Interest is not qualification; establish problem, role, and next
action. Answer questions before pushing for a meeting. Respect refusals and
opt-outs promptly.

Review bounce and complaint signals, replies, qualified conversations, and
opportunities by segment. Open rates can be unreliable. High bounces point first
to data or sending setup; friendly but irrelevant replies point to segment or
message. Stop or revise the sequence when the evidence shows the market or
contact basis is wrong.
