# Turn Relevant Messages into Qualified Conversations

Use this for incoming messages, replies, requested resources, and referrals on
any channel. LinkedIn-specific connection routines and welcome messages live in
the LinkedIn network reference.

## Follow the actual signal

A direct message starts from a question, reaction, requested resource, or
referral. Address that signal before asking about a service. Avoid an
unsolicited pitch disguised as a conversation.

1. **Confirm the reason:** What caught the person's attention?
2. **Understand the situation:** What is happening now, and what have they
   tried?
3. **Explore the problem:** What does the current situation affect?
4. **Clarify the goal:** What would change, and by when?
5. **Check fit:** Can the service help, and are its preconditions present?
6. **Agree on an action:** Offer a call or another useful step only when it
   fits.

Send short messages and usually ask one question at a time. Skip questions
already answered. Respond to what the person actually says instead of forcing a
script. State known prices or a transparent range when asked; explain what is
needed for a precise quote. Request only contact details needed for the agreed
step.

## Conversation prompts

| Stage | Purpose | Possible question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reason | Understand the triggering content | “Which part was relevant to you?” |
| Situation | Place the issue in context | “How does this work for your team today?” |
| Decision | Understand authority | “Would you decide this yourself or with someone else?” |
| Impact | Test the importance | “What happens if this stays the same for another quarter?” |
| Goal | Define a useful next step | “Would a brief call help us answer [specific question]?” |

For consumer services, fit questions may be different. Ask about sensitive
resources only when the answer is necessary and the conversation supports it.

## Branches

- **Curiosity without a clear need:** Give a short explanation, then ask about
  their situation.
- **Specific problem:** Explore impact and timing.
- **“Send information first”:** Send a concise, relevant resource and ask what
  decision it will help them make. Do not flood the person with material.
- **“What does it cost?”:** Answer honestly with the known price or range and
  relevant scope.
- **Existing provider:** Ask whether the goal is being met. Do not manufacture
  dissatisfaction.
- **No time or no reply:** Follow up only when the channel, prior exchange, and
  applicable rules support it; then stop.
- **No need:** Thank the person and close the exchange.
- **Good fit:** Offer a meeting with a clear purpose and preparation.

Success means useful, qualified conversations and suitable meetings, not message
volume.

## Record and send carefully

```text
Conversation trigger:
Stated situation and problem:
Information provided:
Fit and unanswered questions:
Agreed next step:
Owner and date:
```

Before replying to a real message, read the full thread and match the sender's
voice. Verify names, facts, promises, links, and available time slots.

## Two-step resource and conversation

### Step 1: Invite self-identification

Publish a post about a narrow problem and offer a specific additional resource,
such as an assessment, short video, checklist, or report. The person requests it
or asks a related question. The post must be useful on its own; artificial
scarcity and forced comments weaken trust.

Choose a problem in which good-fit prospects recognize themselves. The extra
material should help them diagnose or decide something and connect clearly to
the actual service. State conditions for any result discussed.

```text
Exactly who is this for?
Which recognizable symptom prompts a response?
What does the person receive?
Which new insight should it produce?
Which question can open a useful conversation?
Who would not be a fit for the service?
```

### Step 2: Deliver, then ask

Send the promised material with a brief reference to the request. Ask which part
matters in the person's situation. Use their answer to determine whether to
explain further, ask one clarifying question, or suggest a call. The
conversation should not be a disguised automatic pitch.

Example structure:

- **Post:** “When [specific symptom] appears, [testable cause] may be involved.
  I put together [specific resource] to help check it. Ask for it if that would
  be useful.”
- **First message:** “Here is the resource you asked for. Which part seems
  closest to your situation?”
- **At good fit:** “It sounds as though [specific challenge] is still open.
  Would a short call help us determine [clear purpose]?”

Adapt wording to the relationship, audience, and channel. Do not promise a
result or assume everyone wants an appointment.

### Operating sequence

1. Publish a post or story with one problem and a clearly described resource.
2. Respond personally to genuine requests and deliver what was promised.
3. When appropriate, ask an open question about the person's situation.
4. Assess need and fit. Suggest a call only when a call has a useful purpose.
5. Record the outcome and use repeated questions to improve future content.

A short diagnostic report may suit a technical audience; an everyday example in
video may suit a consumer audience. A professional text network and a visual
platform require different openings. Check current platform rules before using
engagement prompts, automated messages, or audience data.

### Measure the whole path

Record suitable requests, replies, identified needs, meetings, and later
outcomes. Many resource requests without dialogue may mean the resource is too
broad or the follow-up question is unclear. Many poor-fit conversations may mean
the post does not filter for the intended customer. Comments alone are not a
business result.
