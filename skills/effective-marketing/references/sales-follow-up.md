# Follow-up, Existing Clients, and Referrals

## Follow up for a reason

At the first conversation, record what remains open, who decides, and when
another contact would make sense. A follow-up can provide promised information,
support an internal decision, respond to a changed situation, or honor an agreed
date. “Just checking in” without context rarely helps.

1. Read the prior note and current status.
2. Refer to the customer's actual goal or obstacle.
3. Ask whether circumstances or priority have changed.
4. If relevant, agree on the next decision or meeting.
5. Record the result and next date; stop after refusal or loss of fit.

Choose channel and interval for the relationship and local contact rules. A
universal cadence cannot replace an agreed expectation.

| Open reason | Appropriate next action |
| --- | --- |
| Internal decision pending | Record date, participants, and needed material |
| Timing later | Name the event or period likely to change the situation |
| Budget unavailable | Follow up only when there is a plausible future path |
| Objection unresolved | Prepare a specific answer or relevant evidence |
| Missed meeting | Ask what happened; reschedule only if interest remains |
| No need or do-not-contact | Close and honor suppression rules |

## Message patterns

```text
After an agreed internal review:
“We had planned for your team to review [open issue] by [date].
Is a decision possible now, or would another piece of information help?”

When something genuinely changed:
“Since we spoke, [specific, verified change] has occurred.
It may affect your goal of [goal]. Would a brief explanation help?”
```

Adapt any pattern to the real exchange. Avoid manufactured urgency and endless
automated sequences. A CRM view can manage longer cycles, but each individual
contact still needs a defensible reason.

## Existing clients and expansion

Prepare a one-page outcome review: starting point, agreed service, observed
change, open goal, and possible next stage. Ask how valuable the work has been
and what priorities have changed. Offer an extension only when it clearly serves
a new or ongoing need. Repair unmet commitments first.

When a client is satisfied, a specific referral request may be appropriate: What
kind of company or person would benefit, and would an introduction be welcome?
Record permission and outcome. Do not assume the client consents to sharing
another person's details.

## Measure the process

Track the share of open opportunities that received a meaningful next contact,
the number requalified, resulting sales, and recurring reasons for not buying. A
full follow-up list without owners and due tasks is not an operating process.
