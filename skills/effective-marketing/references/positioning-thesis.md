# Positioning Thesis

A positioning thesis explains why a common view of the customer's problem is
incomplete and which alternative mechanism may produce a better outcome. It can
support positioning, content, ads, sales openings, and landing pages. Different
segments may need different theses.

## Build the argument

1. **Problem:** Describe a concrete situation and consequence in customer
   language.
2. **Current belief:** Name the cause or solution the audience commonly assumes.
3. **Counterpoint:** Explain fairly what that belief fails to account for.
4. **Diagnosis:** Identify a more likely cause using observable signals.
5. **Mechanism:** Explain how the proposed approach works and the essential
   steps.
6. **Proof:** Use real cases, data, or demonstrations; otherwise present a
   testable rationale without inventing evidence.
7. **Next step:** Invite an appropriate action, such as a question, assessment,
   or conversation.

The first draft should be explainable in about a minute. Remove repetition, not
causal logic. “We are better” is not a thesis.

## Research worksheet

| Component | Research question |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Which day-to-day situation does the customer describe? |
| Previous attempt | What have they tried repeatedly, and why? |
| Belief | What do they think causes the problem? |
| Counterevidence | Which cases or data challenge that explanation? |
| Diagnosis | What condition must actually be addressed? |
| Mechanism | What changes in practice, and in which order? |
| Proof | Which approved case supports part of the claim? |
| Action | What can the prospect usefully examine next? |

Ask customers what they initially thought caused the problem and what they tried
as a result. Do not invent a mistaken belief merely to create a dramatic
contrast.

## Worked example as a hypothesis

Suppose an IT provider helps small firms implement a CRM. A potential problem
statement is: “Inquiries are lost even though the company already receives
enough leads.” The assumed belief is: “We need more leads.” A counterpoint to
test is: “Some lost revenue occurs between intake, ownership, and follow-up.”
Validate it with actual records: How many inquiries lack an owner? How long
until first contact? The mechanism might be consistent capture, clear stages,
and due tasks. Only use the argument in public copy if the evidence supports it
in the target market.

## Adapt to customer readiness

- **Unaware of the problem:** Start with recognizable symptoms and the cost of
  the status quo. Avoid specialized terms.
- **Aware of the problem, unsure of cause:** Compare a common assumption with
  the diagnosis and its evidence.
- **Comparing solutions:** Show mechanism, conditions, proof, and meaningful
  differences from alternatives.
- **Ready to decide:** Clarify risk, process, customer effort, and next action.

Check whether the right customer recognizes the problem immediately, the belief
is fairly represented, the diagnosis follows from the symptoms, and the team can
deliver what the thesis implies. If the argument collapses, revisit the customer
and problem definition. More copy cannot rescue weak causality.

## Express the thesis by channel

A useful default sequence is **situation or symptom → common response → new
insight → short proof or example → next step**. Change the sequence when the
audience or channel requires it.

| Channel | Lead with | Check before release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ad | Recognizable situation and one reason to act | Visual and copy agree; next event is clear |
| Social post | One insight and a concrete example | Call to action fits the post's intent |
| Landing page | Problem and desired outcome | Mechanism and proof precede the form |
| Lead resource | A narrow buying-relevant question | Title promises a specific insight |
| Video | Title and first seconds answer the same question | The rest of the video actually delivers it |

**Problem-led post:** “Many [target customers] experience [visible symptom]. The
usual response is [attempt]. That helps when [condition]. First check
[diagnostic question]. If [signal] appears, [mechanism] may be the next useful
step. [Context-appropriate action].”

**Decision-focused resource:** Combine customer role, concrete problem, and the
insight that enables a choice. For example, “How [role] can tell whether
[process] is failing at [bottleneck].” It should help diagnosis and
decision-making; it need not reproduce the full paid engagement.
