# Route: LinkedIn Posts

Write or revise LinkedIn posts, develop ideas, or plan a content calendar from
the user's audience, voice, goal, and evidence. Reuse an existing marketing,
selling, or cross-platform brief; ask only for missing context that materially
changes the requested result.

## Drafting

- Preserve the strongest grounded idea and the user's natural phrasing. Make
  the opening specific, keep paragraphs readable, and end when the thought is
  complete. A question, hook formula, or call to action is optional.
- Never invent personal experience, vulnerability, customer quotations,
  numbers, endorsements, or outcomes. Use a supportable position instead of
  manufactured drama or engagement bait.
- Preserve verified technical behavior, limits, qualifications, and exact
  product, protocol, API, and technology names. Simplify explanation without
  erasing necessary distinctions.
- A technical topic does not turn a post into controlled documentation. Keep
  natural channel language; a named controlled-language contract governs only
  the artifact or embedded text it actually covers. Use `effective-delivery`
  for that contract and source verification.
- Apply recorded brand or editorial guidance when relevant. Durable changes to
  audience relationship, voice, or claims belong in the project's decision
  records, not a new LinkedIn-specific memory file.

Return the requested finished post or revision first. Add alternatives,
distribution hypotheses, or media suggestions only when requested or needed to
resolve a meaningful choice. A single-post request does not need an intake,
category search, calendar, or acquisition-strategy exercise.

## Formats and platform details

Choose the format from the material: text for a single idea, a document for a
sequence that needs pages, a poll for focused input, video for demonstration,
or an infographic for useful data or comparisons. Include captions and
accessible media where applicable.

Use hashtags only when they help discovery or understanding. Choose cadence
from audience needs, capacity, and account evidence. Verify current official
LinkedIn requirements when exact limits, publishing formats, or platform
mechanics affect the task; do not turn a fixed frequency, response window,
hashtag count, or format into an algorithm guarantee.
