# Measure and Improve Campaigns

## Follow the entire chain

Track spend → relevant reach → click or chat → valid inquiry → qualified meeting
→ attended meeting → sale → delivery result. The most important loss often
occurs after the lead enters the CRM, not inside the ad platform.

For each campaign and period, record spend, reach, impressions, clicks or chats,
valid inquiries, qualified contacts, booked meetings, attended meetings,
proposals, and sales. Define every stage and its data source. Separate paid from
organic and new from returning where the system can do so reliably.

Calculate only metrics with a known numerator and denominator:

- **Cost per valid lead = spend / valid leads.**
- **Qualification rate = qualified contacts / valid leads.**
- **Attendance rate = attended meetings / booked meetings.**
- **Close rate = sales / completed sales conversations.**

Show absolute counts with rates. A percentage based on three leads is not a
stable forecast. Cheap leads are not a success if they do not become suitable
customers.

## Diagnose in sequence

1. **Technical delivery:** Is the campaign approved and delivering? Do links,
   forms, events, and CRM handoffs work? Are sources and periods consistent?
2. **Attention:** Is the audience suitable, and do people understand the opening
   and offer? Assess creative and targeting together.
3. **Inquiry:** Does the destination match the promise? Is the next action clear
   and the form usable?
4. **Qualification:** Why are inquiries unsuitable? Do the same false
   expectations recur from the ad?
5. **Sales:** Are prospects contacted promptly, attending meetings, and
   receiving a coherent offer?
6. **Economics:** Compare cost through sale with contribution margin and
   delivery capacity when the figures exist.

| Symptom | First check |
| --- | --- |
| Little delivery | Account, approvals, audience, budget, bidding |
| Clicks without inquiries | Message, page speed, destination, form |
| Many poor-fit leads | Targeting, promise, qualification questions |
| Meetings without sales | Qualification, expectations, conversation, offer |
| Declining performance | Creative fatigue, saturation, changed competition |

Fix the earliest demonstrated weak transition. Distinguish technical faults from
message problems. Change one meaningful variable at a time and log the
hypothesis, variant, launch date, expected signal, and result. A new creative
should test a new opening or proof point; recoloring alone rarely provides a
useful insight. Use sensible comparison periods and consider seasonality. Mark
decisions as provisional when tracking is incomplete.

## One-page decision report

State the period and objective, metric chain, weakest transition, most likely
cause and supporting observation, one next change, owner, and review date. Note
missing data and how it will be recovered. End with a decision: continue, adjust
budget, replace creative, repair the form, or revise offer and message.
