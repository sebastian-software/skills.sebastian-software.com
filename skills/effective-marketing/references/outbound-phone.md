# Phone Outreach

## Market gate before calling

Determine the prospect's jurisdiction, whether the number is business or
consumer, the call type, the relationship, and any applicable consent,
suppression, or do-not-call rules. Check current official guidance and company
policy before launching. A script does not make an impermissible call
acceptable.

## Prepare the conversation

Choose a specific reason to contact this segment. Write branches for
receptionist or assistant, buyer, lack of time, lack of need, existing provider,
and an appropriate next step. Label statements that are proven versus hypotheses
to check. Practice for clarity and calm tone, not artificial familiarity.

A decision-maker conversation has five parts: **opening, needs questions,
relevance check, brief explanation, and appointment or clear follow-up**.

```text
Opening: “Hi [name], I'm calling about [specific and truthful reason].
Is now a reasonable time for one quick question?”

Need: “How are you handling [specific situation] today?”
Follow-up: “What is working, and where does it create extra work or loss?”

When a real need emerges: “I ask because we help [customer type]
address [problem]. A short conversation could establish whether
[approach] fits your situation. Would [specific option] work?”

When it does not fit: “Understood. It sounds like this is not relevant
right now. Thanks for letting me know.”
```

This is a structure, not universal wording. Ask another question when an answer
is unclear. If the prospect already has a provider, find out whether the goal is
being met; do not invent a shortcoming. Tell an assistant the actual reason for
the call. Respect a refusal and record it in the suppression process.

## CRM outcomes

Separate no answer, assistant reached, buyer reached, no need, interest,
qualified meeting, agreed callback, and objection to further contact. Every open
outcome needs an owner and next date. A do-not-contact request requires a
reliable exclusion rule. These statuses let the team distinguish data quality
from message quality.

## Improve the script

Track reach, interest, and qualified meetings across enough comparable attempts
to learn. Version the opening, diagnostic questions, and offer separately. If
buyers are reached but do not recognize the problem, revisit segment and
language. If they show interest but do not book, examine the purpose and handoff
to the next conversation. If meetings are poor fit, improve qualification rather
than simply adding call volume.

Role-play an assistant, a rushed buyer, an existing vendor, and genuine lack of
need. After each run, ask: Was the reason credible? Was the problem actually
checked? Was the next step clear?
