# Emotion and Behavior

Marketing psychology is useful when it explains a reader state and leads to a
truthful, testable writing choice. It is harmful when a bias name becomes
permission to intensify pressure, conceal information, or exploit vulnerability.

## Start with the state, not the tactic

Describe:

```text
eligible reader + current situation + intended progress + present barrier
+ proposed mechanism + honest prompt + guardrail
```

Ask whether the barrier is:

- insufficient relevance or motivation;
- low confidence in the claim, organization, or outcome;
- poor understanding of the category, mechanism, fit, or next step;
- practical friction, effort, cost, or missing capability;
- fear of switching, error, exposure, loss, or regret;
- social or identity tension;
- bad timing or no legitimate reason to act now.

Copy can clarify relevance, confidence, understanding, and expectation. It
cannot remove a real product defect, unaffordable price, inaccessible flow,
missing permission, or operational burden by describing it more aggressively.

## Create truthful emotional resonance

Emotion is already present in consequential choices. Find it in evidence:

- **Recognition:** a concrete moment, task, interruption, workaround, or phrase
  makes the reader feel understood.
- **Stakes:** show what the current situation costs in time, attention,
  confidence, opportunity, money, or relationships without exaggeration.
- **Mental simulation:** make the future workflow or experience easy to imagine
  through observable detail.
- **Relief:** name uncertainty, repetition, exposure, or effort that genuinely
  becomes smaller.
- **Agency:** show what the reader can understand, choose, control, reverse, or
  accomplish.
- **Identity:** connect to a real role, craft, value, or group only when the
  relationship is relevant and inclusive.
- **Aspiration:** describe credible progress, not a fantasy transformation the
  offer cannot support.

Prefer a supplied customer moment, product demonstration, or accurate
before-and-after process to generic fear, hype, sensory decoration, or a
fictional story presented as typical.

## Apply behavioral mechanisms responsibly

Use a mechanism only after recording:

| Question | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Reader state | Who encounters which decision or barrier? |
| Intended effect | What understanding, confidence, or action should change? |
| Evidence basis | What observation supports this mechanism here? |
| Truthful trigger | Which real fact, choice, or prompt expresses it? |
| Ethical boundary | What must remain visible, voluntary, and reversible? |
| Backfire risk | How could this reduce trust, fit, comprehension, or welfare? |
| Test or guardrail | What evidence would support, reject, or limit its use? |

Useful applications include:

- **Social proof:** show attributed and relevant evidence that people in a
  comparable situation made or benefited from the choice.
- **Authority:** expose the specific expertise, standard, method, or independent
  validation relevant to the claim.
- **Commitment and progress:** make an already chosen process understandable and
  show genuine completion, without escalating commitment by surprise.
- **Familiarity:** use consistent category, product, and action language across
  touchpoints.
- **Loss and regret:** explain a real consequence or reversibility condition,
  not a speculative catastrophe.
- **Scarcity and timing:** disclose a real material constraint, capacity, stock,
  deadline, or event with its conditions.
- **Defaults and choice structure:** describe the recommended fit and
  alternatives transparently; never hide consent, cost, renewal, or refusal.
- **Ease and prompting:** make the next legitimate step concrete, timely, and
  easy to understand without disguising its commitment.
- **Framing and contrast:** choose an accurate comparison frame and preserve
  denominators, baselines, and conditions that could change interpretation.

Do not claim that a named bias guarantees a result. Many effects are contextual,
and a transparent explanation may outperform a tactic that readers recognize
as manipulation.

## Match emotion to risk

- For low-risk, experiential offers, vivid anticipation and identity may carry
  more of the path.
- For utilitarian offers, combine recognition with mechanism, concrete
  consequences, and demonstrable proof.
- For expensive, sensitive, regulated, or hard-to-reverse choices, reduce
  intensity and increase conditions, comparison, uncertainty, control, and
  proof.
- For vulnerable audiences or moments, avoid exploiting fear, isolation,
  illness, financial distress, grief, dependency, or limited ability to refuse.

If the copy needs hidden information, forced continuity, deceptive interface
behavior, or escalating anxiety to work, reject the tactic and route the
underlying offer, product, or funnel problem to its owner.

## Emotional and ethical check

- Is the feeling grounded in an observed or inherently plausible situation?
- Does the copy increase agency and comprehension as well as motivation?
- Can the reader inspect the claim and decline the next step without penalty?
- Is social proof attributable, relevant, permitted, and non-synthetic?
- Are urgency, scarcity, savings, and comparisons materially true?
- Does identity language welcome a relevant reader without shaming outsiders?
- Are high-risk audiences given more context and control, not more pressure?
- Is the proposed mechanism a hypothesis with a guardrail rather than a
  promised conversion effect?
